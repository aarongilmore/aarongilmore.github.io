"What do I believe in?”. We all ask ourselves this question at some point in our lives. In the late Wendell Berry’s <sup id="fnref:1"><a href="#fn:1" class="footnote">1</a></sup> essay “Why I Am not Going To Buy A Computer” <sup id="fnref:2"><a href="#fn:2" class="footnote">2</a></sup> he outlined his own beliefs and, throughout the essay, why they mattered to him:

> [...] peace, economic justice, ecological health, political honesty, family and community stability, good work.

I think most people would align with these beliefs, myself included. What I would add or expand on is:

- The natural world, conservation, and ecological morality
- Culture and art
- Family, friendship, and community

Like Berry, I do not see that AI is bringing us one step nearer to anything that does matter to me. It is well established that AI data centres consume and pollute, the industries they power are innately political, and—speaking from a design perspective—the work is no longer good.

But I’m not naive, I know AI has some fantastic applications. I think being anti-AI because of ecological reasons is in many ways hypocritical (see: water use in dairy farming, powering Meta’s servers, using contactless payments)

> Like almost everybody else, I am hooked to the energy corporations, which I do not admire. I hope to become less hooked to them. In my work, I try to be as little hooked to them as possible.

It could read: “Like almost everybody else, I am hooked to the AI corporations, which I do not admire[...]”
