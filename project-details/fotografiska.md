### Introduction & Context
Fotografiska is a global photography museum with spaces in cities including Stockholm, New York, Tallinn, Berlin and Shanghai. As the organisation prepared to open its Berlin location, we worked with the team to rethink its wider digital presence.

This meant relaunching the global website, creating flexible experiences for each location and establishing a foundation that could support future growth. The aim was to give the artists and exhibitions more space online while making practical tasks—from planning a visit to becoming a member—feel part of one coherent experience.

### My Role
I worked across several parts of the experience, designing flexible content modules and the visit page, while leading the design of the membership flow—from discovering the benefits through to joining. The process needed to feel clear and consistent while working across Fotografiska’s different locations.

### Design Approach
As a photography museum, it was important that the work remained the main focus. We designed a restrained, flexible system that gave exhibitions, artists and imagery room to lead, while making practical information easy to find and creating a familiar experience across every city.

We also wanted the site to be more than a companion to the physical museums. It needed to function as an online archive in its own right—one that could preserve and surface Fotografiska’s programme beyond the limits of bricks and mortar.

### Takeaways
The project showed how a digital platform can support a physical experience without being limited by it. By establishing shared patterns across cities, we made practical journeys such as planning a visit and becoming a member feel familiar, while leaving space for each location to retain its own character.

The same system gave exhibitions, artists and photography a longer life online—creating a foundation for an archive that could continue to grow alongside Fotografiska’s programme.
