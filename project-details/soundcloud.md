### Introduction & Context

After years of incremental product changes, SoundCloud’s web and mobile experiences needed a clearer and more consistent foundation. We partnered with its product teams to develop a new design language that could work across every platform and support millions of creators and listeners around the world.

The challenge was to bring structure to a large and complex product without losing the openness and character that made SoundCloud distinctive in the first place.

### My Role

SoundCloud was my first project at A Color Bright. I worked on scaling the design system into a foundation that could support the product across the web app and iPad, in both light and dark modes.

I delivered key reference views that helped define how the system would work in practice. My focus included some of SoundCloud’s most important experiences—the player, profiles, streams and feeds—ensuring that shared patterns remained consistent while adapting to the needs of each part of the product.

### Design Approach

We took a system-led approach, but developed it through real product experiences rather than designing components in isolation. Key reference views allowed us to test the visual language against the complexity of the player, profiles, streams and feeds, refining the system as new needs emerged.

The aim was to create consistency without making every part of the product feel the same. Patterns needed to work across screen sizes, platforms and light and dark modes, while leaving enough flexibility for different types of content and interaction. This gave teams a shared foundation they could apply with confidence as the product continued to evolve.

### Takeaways

The project showed me that a design system becomes useful through application. Building key screens alongside the system exposed gaps early and gave product teams clear examples of how its principles should translate into real experiences.

It also reinforced the importance of balancing consistency with character. SoundCloud needed a coherent experience across platforms and themes, but it still had to feel open, expressive and recognisably its own. That balance created a stronger foundation for the product without losing what made it distinctive.
